Including the donations of foreign Roman Catholic powers, the income exceeds 800,000l. sterling.

Notwithstanding, the expences exceed the revenue, and the debt of the Papal Chair is continually encreasing, and now amounts to above 10,000,000l. which pays an interest of 8 per cent. As the Catholic Princes grow every day more and more frugal, the capital of this debt will, in all probability, rise in proportion to the diminution of the revenue, if the See of Rome does not take different measures from what it has hitherto done.		PEPIN and CHARLES give the Popes the greatest part of the Exarchate, 774. The ceremony of kissing the Pope's foot as early as this period. Commencement of the Schism between the Eastern and Western Church, 794. JOHN VIII. becomes master of Capua. The Clergy forbid to marry before this time. LEO IX. obtains possession of Benevento, 1052. The destructive crusades commence, 1096. GREGORY VII. raises the Papal power to its greatest height, 1077. An universal corruption of manners prevails. Origin of the orders of Carthusians, Cistercians, and that of St. John. Order of the Knights Templars abolished, and the Master of the Order, James Molai, burned alive, 1307. The Knights of St. John conquer the Island of Rhodes. CLEMENT V. obtains possession of Avignon, 1348. Hufs burned, in 1415, by order of the Council of CONSTANCE. INNOCENT III. makes himself master of Rome, 1393. JULIUS II. acquires Bologna, 1513. LUTHER shakes the power of the Roman Pontiff, and commences the reformation, 1517. Malta given to the Knights of St. John, 1530. CLEMENT VII. obtains possession of the Marquisate of Ancona, 1532. Institution of the Order of Jesuits, and Ignatius Loyola, 1534. SIXTUS V. 1585. CLEMENT VIII. subjects Ferrara, 1598. URBAN VIII. acquires Urbino, 1626. INNOCENT X. annexes Castro and Ronciglione to his dominions. ALEXANDER VII. receives Christiana, Queen of Sweden, 1655. CLEMENT XIV. suppresses the Order of Jesuits; makes amends for the faults of his predecessors, and dies Sept. 24, 1774. PIUS VI. becomes Pope, 1775, visits Vienna, 1782, and endeavours to maintain the respectability of the Papal chair by prudent measures. The power of the Pope overturned by the French, who had before taken possession of Avignon, 1798. PIUS VI. dies, 1799.The government is despotic. The highest college of government in the Papal dominions is the Consistory, in which all the concerns of the See of Rome are transacted by the Cardinals, who hold the highest offices of state. The laws of the Pope are termed Bulls. The see of Rome is at present vacant. The Pope, like all the Roman Catholic clergy, is unmarried, and is entitled His Holiness; the cardinals are styled Eminence.The succession is elective. The person elected must be a cardinal, at least fifty five years of age, and a native of Italy. Eleven days after the decease of a pope a sermon is preached to the cardinals, in St. Peter's church, on the duties of election; they then go into the conclave, where the pope is elected by two-thirds of the votes; but his election is generally dependent on the Emperor of Germany and the House of Bourbon. The new pope is carried, amidst the ringing of bells and the discharge of great and small artillery, to St. Peter's church, and eight days afterwards is crowned with the triple crown with the greatest solemnity.The Roman Catholic is the established religion; but all other religious persuasions, particularly Jews, enjoy certain privileges: the spirit of toleration prevails here in general in a greater degree than in many other Catholic countries. Exterior ceremonies occupy the place of religion; the clergy, whole number exceeds 100,000, keep the wretched inhabitants in the blindest superstition, frequently themselves turn their practices to ridicule, are guilty of the greatest immoralities, are often many degrees beyond indifference in matters relating to religion, which not rarely serves as a protection even to the vilest miscreants.
